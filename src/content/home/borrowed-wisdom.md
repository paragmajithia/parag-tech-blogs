Over the years I've kept short, personal notes on books that changed how I
think about money, focus, and building a life on my own terms. This is a
running collection — seven so far, more added as I keep reading. Each one
below is condensed to the single reframe that stuck with me most — the full
PDF has the frameworks, tactics, and quick-reference tables behind each one,
if you want to go deeper.

## Book by Book

### 1. The 4-Hour Work Week — Tim Ferriss

> The goal isn't working less for its own sake — it's ruthlessly maximizing
> the ratio of results to hours spent, then deliberately choosing what to do
> with the time that frees up.

**Takeaway:** Design your work around a small set of high-leverage
activities, automate or delegate everything else, and use the time this
frees up deliberately — instead of waiting for a retirement that may never
feel like enough.

---

### 2. Rich Dad Poor Dad — Robert Kiyosaki

> Your house is not an asset if it's where you live and costs you money
> every month — real wealth comes from acquiring things that generate
> income, then living off that income.

**Takeaway:** Focus less on how much you earn and more on what you do with
it — acquiring assets that generate income is what compounds into long-term
financial independence.

---

### 3. The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People — Stephen R. Covey

> Effectiveness starts inward — with how you see your own choices, values,
> and priorities — before it can extend outward to relationships and teams.

**Takeaway:** Real effectiveness is built from the inside out — private
self-mastery first, then trust-based collaboration with others, sustained by
ongoing self-renewal.

---

### 4. Deep Work — Cal Newport

> High-quality work produced = time spent × intensity of focus. Most people
> maximize the wrong variable — logging hours — instead of raising the
> intensity of focus within those hours.

**Takeaway:** Treat sustained, undistracted focus as a skill to train
deliberately — scheduling it, protecting it from shallow work, and
practicing boredom tolerance — because it's increasingly rare and valuable.

---

### 5. The Psychology of Money — Morgan Housel

> Financial success is a soft skill about behavior, not a hard science about
> formulas — doing reasonably well consistently beats being technically
> right occasionally.

**Takeaway:** Manage your own behavior, temperament, and time horizon well,
and it will matter more for your financial life than almost any technical
investing skill.

---

### 6. Extreme Ownership — Jocko Willink & Leif Babin

> It's not what you preach, it's what you tolerate — a leader's true
> standards are revealed by what they let slide, not by what they say out
> loud.

**Takeaway:** Take full ownership of outcomes — good and bad — set ego
aside, simplify your plans, and push decision-making down to where the
problem actually lives.

---

### 7. The Lean Startup — Eric Ries

> A startup's job isn't executing a plan — it's a search for a repeatable,
> scalable business model, and that search is only efficient if learning
> happens as fast as possible.

**Takeaway:** Treat a new product or venture as a series of testable
hypotheses — build the smallest experiment possible, measure it against a
real decision, and let evidence decide whether to pivot or press on.
